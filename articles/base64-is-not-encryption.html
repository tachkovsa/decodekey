Base64 is not encryption

November 2026 · 3 min read

Base64 turns arbitrary bytes into a string of letters, digits, +, / and padding. It exists so binary data can travel through text-only channels: JSON, email headers, URLs, config files.

It is reversible by design

There is no key and no secret. Anyone who sees a Base64 string can decode it in one step — including our Base64 tool. That is the point: encoding is not protection.

echo 'c2VjcmV0' | base64 -d
secret

Where the confusion comes from

Encoded strings look opaque. People paste them into tickets, logs and chat, assuming they are safe because they are unreadable at a glance. They are not: credentials, tokens and personal data encoded this way leak exactly as if they were plain text.

URL-safe variant

Standard Base64 uses + and /, which have meaning in URLs. JWT and many APIs use the URL-safe alphabet instead: - and _, usually without padding. That is why a token segment can look slightly different from a regular Base64 blob.

What to use instead

  • For confidentiality: real encryption (AES-GCM, ChaCha20-Poly1305) with keys stored outside the data.
  • For integrity: signatures or MACs, verified on the server.
  • For transport: Base64 is fine — just do not call it security.

A useful rule: if you would not paste the raw value into a public chat, do not paste its Base64 either.
